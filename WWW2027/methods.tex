\subsection{Problem Formulation and Overview}
\label{sec:formulation}
Let $\mathcal{V}$ denote the candidate video catalog and
$\mathcal{H}_u=(v_{u,1},\ldots,v_{u,L_u})$ the observed interaction history of user $u$.
Our goal is to rank candidate items for the user's next interaction.
HGVRec learns a content representation $\mathbf{z}_v\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ for each video and a user representation $\mathbf{q}_u\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ from the history, with score
\begin{equation}
  f(u,v)=\mathbf{q}_u^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{z}_v.
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
The same video encoder supplies history-item and candidate-item representations.

We observe each video through temporally ordered windows of sampled frames.
We call an observation window a \emph{scene}; this does not imply a detected shot or a ground-truth action boundary.
A frozen MLLM produces a scene graph containing entities, action descriptions, action-specific role assignments, and scene context.
After structural validation, video $v$ has $S_v$ valid scenes with original window indices $p_1<\cdots<p_{S_v}$.
Retaining these indices prevents a failed or missing window from silently joining two otherwise separated action segments.
An entity has a textual name and attributes; an action has a textual phrase and actor, target, tool, and place assignments.
Context is serialized from scene-level format, medium, and topic fields.
Entity identifiers specify local graph connectivity and are excluded from text embedding inputs.
The video title enters the representation stage as a separate input after visual extraction.

Figure~\ref{fig:hgvrec-framework} summarizes the computation:
REAF constructs role-aware action features, a residual scene encoder produces scene vectors, ASHA organizes them into a video representation, and a sequential recommender models the user's interaction history.
The MLLM outputs and their text embeddings remain fixed during recommendation learning.
We introduce ASHA first, taking scene vectors as inputs, and then define their construction through REAF.
Throughout, $[\cdot\,;\,\cdot]$ denotes concatenation and $\sigma$ denotes ReLU.
Figure~\ref{fig:hgvrec-motivation} illustrates the two levels of structure using a cooking example.

\subsection{Action-Segment Hypergraph Aggregation (ASHA)}
\label{sec:asha}
\paragraph{Action keys and contiguous segments.}
For a fixed video, let $\mathbf{x}_s\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ be the representation of valid scene $s$ and let $\mathcal{A}_s$ be its actions.
A deterministic function $\kappa$ converts an action phrase into a consistency key.
The implementation lowercases and tokenizes the phrase, removes participant markers and a fixed set of function words, and normalizes the first remaining token using irregular-form mappings and suffix rules.
Empty or unknown phrases produce no key.
This is a lightweight lexical rule, not a semantic equivalence test; for example, it does not merge arbitrary synonymous actions.
We use the set of keys in a scene,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{K}_s=\{\kappa(a):a\in\mathcal{A}_s,\ \kappa(a)\neq\varnothing\}.
  \label{eq:keys}
\end{equation}
Multiple actions with the same key in one scene contribute one membership.

For key $k$, an action segment $e$ is a maximal run of scenes satisfying both $k\in\mathcal{K}_s$ and consecutiveness of their original indices.
Specifically, adjacent members $s,s+1$ must satisfy $p_{s+1}=p_s+1$.
A missing window, a scene without $k$, or a later reappearance of $k$ separates segments.
Singleton segments are retained.
A scene containing several keys belongs to several segments, allowing overlapping actions to induce different aggregation groups.
Neither participant identity nor temporal identity of entities is required to construct these groups.
Thus, a segment expresses continuity of an extracted action key across observations rather than a verified continuous real-world event.

\paragraph{Hypergraph construction.}
The hypergraph contains one node per valid scene. Its $K$ hyperedges are the action segments, collected in $\mathcal{E}$.
Its incidence matrix and associated counts are
\begin{equation}
  H_{sk}=\mathbb{I}[s\in e_k],\qquad
  n_k=\sum_{s=1}^{S}H_{sk},\qquad
  d_s=\sum_{k=1}^{K}H_{sk},
  \label{eq:incidence}
\end{equation}
where $S=S_v$ and $n_k\geq 1$ for every constructed segment.
The incidence structure is fixed before recommendation training.
It explicitly records which action key groups which scene indices, providing an inspectable grouping mechanism.
This structural property alone does not establish that a group is a faithful explanation of a recommendation.

\paragraph{Scene-to-segment aggregation.}
Following the two-stage aggregation framework of AllSet~\cite{chien2022allset}, we first pool the content of each segment and augment it with its extent:
\begin{align}
  \overline{\mathbf{x}}_k &= \frac{1}{n_k}\sum_{s=1}^{S}H_{sk}\mathbf{x}_s, \\
  \mathbf{l}_k &= [\log n_k\,;\,n_k/S], \\
  \mathbf{u}_k &= \sigma\!\left(W_{\mathrm{seg}}
      [\overline{\mathbf{x}}_k\,;\,\mathbf{l}_k]
      +\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{seg}}\right).
  \label{eq:segment}
\end{align}
Here $W_{\mathrm{seg}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times(D+2)}$.
Mean pooling summarizes segment content, while $\mathbf{l}_k$ preserves information that the mean alone discards.
Its entries represent the number of member scenes and its fraction of all valid scenes in the video; neither entry is a duration in seconds.

\paragraph{Segment-to-scene update.}
A scene receives the mean of the representations of its incident segments:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{m}_s &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K}H_{sk}\mathbf{u}_k}{\max(1,d_s)}, \\
  \mathbf{x}'_s &= \mathbf{x}_s + \alpha\,\mathbb{I}[d_s>0]\,
       \sigma\!\left(W_{\mathrm{msg}}[\mathbf{x}_s\,;\,\mathbf{m}_s]
       +\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{msg}}\right),
  \label{eq:scene-update}
\end{align}
where $W_{\mathrm{msg}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times 2D}$.
Including the receiving scene's own representation allows the update to depend on both local content and segment context.
Scenes without valid action keys have $d_s=0$ and retain $\mathbf{x}_s$.
We use one scene-to-segment-to-scene pass.

\paragraph{Video readout.}
ASHA combines the mean of updated scenes with a separate segment contribution:
\begin{align}
  \overline{\mathbf{u}} &=
      \frac{1}{\max(1,K)}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{u}_k, \\
  \mathbf{z}_v &= \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathbf{x}'_s
      +\beta\,\mathbb{I}[K>0]W_{\mathrm{out}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}.
  \label{eq:video-readout}
\end{align}
$W_{\mathrm{out}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}$ has no bias.
The readout weights segments equally, with segment size already supplied to their encoder in Equation~\eqref{eq:segment}.
Both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are unconstrained trainable scalars initialized to zero; they are shared across videos and are not sigmoid probabilities.
At initialization, ASHA therefore reduces to a mean of its input scene vectors.
If $K=0$, its output remains the scene mean.
The $S=0$ case is handled by the title fallback described below.

\subsection{Role-aware Entity--Action Fusion (REAF)}
\label{sec:reaf}
\paragraph{Typed features and role assignments.}
Let $\phi(\cdot)\in\mathbb{R}^{F}$ be a frozen text encoder.
We independently project the video title $t_v$, entity description $e$, action phrase $a$, and scene context $c_s$ into $d$-dimensional features:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathbf{t}_v &= W_t\phi(t_v)+\mathbf{b}_t,&
  \mathbf{e}_{s,e} &= W_e\phi(e)+\mathbf{b}_e,\\
  \mathbf{a}_{s,a} &= W_a\phi(a)+\mathbf{b}_a,&
  \mathbf{c}_s &= W_c\phi(c_s)+\mathbf{b}_c.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:typed-projections}
\end{equation}
The entity and action expressions apply to every entity and action in scene $s$.
Missing titles are explicitly masked to zero after projection.
Let $\mathcal{R}$ contain four role types: actor, target, tool, and place.
For each action $a$ and role $r\in\mathcal{R}$, we obtain a role vector
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{g}_{s,a,r}=\begin{cases}
    \mathbf{e}_{s,r(a)}, & \text{if role $r$ links to entity $r(a)$},\\
    \mathbf{b}_{r}^{\mathrm{abs}}, & \text{if role $r$ is absent},\\
    \mathbf{b}_{r}^{\mathrm{unk}}, & \text{if role $r$ is unknown}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:role-input}
\end{equation}
The two state vectors are learned separately for each role.
An absent role and an unknown participant therefore need not receive the same representation.
REAF uses the extracted assignments; it does not infer new role links or track an entity across scenes.

\paragraph{Action update and scene construction.}
The residual update of action $a$ in scene $s$ is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathbf{r}_{s,a} &=[\mathbf{g}_{s,a,\mathrm{actor}}\,;
       \mathbf{g}_{s,a,\mathrm{target}}\,;
       \mathbf{g}_{s,a,\mathrm{tool}}\,;
       \mathbf{g}_{s,a,\mathrm{place}}],\\
  \widetilde{\mathbf{a}}_{s,a} &= \mathbf{a}_{s,a}
      +\sigma\!\left(W_R[\mathbf{a}_{s,a}\,;\,\mathbf{r}_{s,a}]
      +\mathbf{b}_R\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reaf}
\end{equation}
with $W_R\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times 5d}$.
The fixed slot order permits different transformations of the same entity feature when it serves different roles.
Entity features themselves are unchanged by this update.

Let $\overline{\mathbf{e}}_s$ be the mean of projected entity features and $\overline{\mathbf{a}}_s$ the mean of updated action features in scene $s$.
The mean of an empty entity or action set is a zero vector.
We construct the scene representation as
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{b}_s &=[\mathbf{t}_v\,;\,\overline{\mathbf{e}}_s\,;
                   \overline{\mathbf{a}}_s\,;\,\mathbf{c}_s],\\
  \mathbf{x}_s &= \mathbf{b}_s+\sigma(W_{\mathrm{scene}}\mathbf{b}_s
                                     +\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{scene}}),
  \label{eq:scene-encoder}
\end{align}
where $D=4d$ and $W_{\mathrm{scene}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}$.
This single-layer residual MLP operates independently on each scene.
REAF encodes local participation before pooling actions, while ASHA subsequently models groups of scenes.

\paragraph{Missing observations.}
Malformed or failed scene outputs do not enter the graph encoder.
A valid scene with no actions still contributes its available title, entity, and context information to the scene mean.
If a video has no valid scenes, we apply the same residual scene MLP to $[\mathbf{t}_v;\mathbf{0};\mathbf{0};\mathbf{0}]$.
If neither a title nor a valid scene is available, its final video vector is masked to zero.
These rules preserve the recommendation catalog despite extraction failures.
They define fallback behavior rather than repair missing visual evidence.

\subsection{Sequential Recommendation and Joint Optimization}
\label{sec:objective}
For each prediction event, we retain the most recent $L$ available history items, add learned position embeddings to their video vectors, and apply a causally masked SASRec-style Transformer~\cite{kang2018sasrec}.
Padding positions are masked.
The final normalized hidden state at the last valid position is passed through a two-layer residual user MLP with LayerNorm and GELU to obtain $\mathbf{q}_u$.
History positions are modeled chronologically at the user level, independently of ASHA's organization of scenes \emph{inside} an item.
Candidate scores follow Equation~\eqref{eq:score}; there is no learned item-ID embedding table in the content encoder.

\paragraph{Popularity-corrected in-batch objective.}
A training batch contains $B$ history--target pairs $(\mathcal{H}_i,y_i)$.
The targets of other batch examples serve as negatives.
Let $Q(v)$ be the normalized frequency of $v$ as a target among training transitions with nonempty histories; validation and test targets are excluded from this frequency estimate.
The corrected logit for user-history row $i$ and target column $j$ is
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{ij}=\mathbf{q}_i^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{z}_{y_j}-\log Q(y_j).
  \label{eq:corrected-logits}
\end{equation}
For $j\neq i$, we mask column $j$ if $y_j=y_i$ or $y_j$ occurs in the truncated input history $\mathcal{H}_i$.
The diagonal positive is always retained.
Writing $\widetilde{\ell}_{ij}$ for the resulting logits, the objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}
       \log\frac{\exp(\widetilde{\ell}_{ii})}
       {\sum_{j=1}^{B}\exp(\widetilde{\ell}_{ij})}.
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
The implementation sets masked logits to $-10^4$.
The popularity correction is used during training; evaluation ranks the uncorrected dot-product scores.
All typed projections, role-state embeddings, REAF, the scene MLP, ASHA, the sequential encoder, and the user MLP are optimized jointly.
The frozen MLLM and text encoder receive no recommendation gradients, and action-segment memberships are not learned.
